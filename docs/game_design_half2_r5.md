# 一笔一画: Round 5 design, second half (1:26.4–3:27)

Status: **DECIDED by Claude (Fable 5.1) on 2026-10-17, on Jade's delegation** ("you have artistic freedom to change anything you
want in the second half"; "build your vision"). This doc supersedes the matching beats of `game_design_acts3-5.md` and
`half2_cinematic_pass.md`. Anything not mentioned here keeps what half2_v3 has. Times are song seconds; the half2 review files
start at 86.385 (file time + 86.385 = song time). Measured cues win over `timing.json` (final slam 139.395, drop-2 stops ~0.4 s early).

Each ★ item carries a cost tag: **[staging]** = camera, placement, acting and timing on existing assets and APIs;
**[logic]** = new scene logic or creature behaviour; **[assets]** = a new model, decal or prop; **[art-dir]** = look, grade, lens.

## Thesis: legibility and stakes, not fidelity and not cleverness

Half 1 is lower fidelity than half 2 and it works, because every room has a readable rule, visible danger, and a solution that
lands on a beat. Half 2's weak stretch (86–107) has none of those: nothing threatens the party, and the viewer can't tell what
the goal is before it's solved. The native heroes at 2.5 already match the keyframes' voxel scale; what separates the render
from k08b is composition and light, not count. And a puzzle is only fun to *watch* if the viewer forms a hypothesis before the
characters act, which at 140 BPM means one idea per room. Talos stays as vocabulary and theme (connectors, receivers, plates,
fields, a sunlit garden that turns out to be code), not as pacing.

Three rules for every scene in this half:

1. **Show the lock before the tools.** Each room opens on one wide shot from the party's side holding a full bar: the goal, the
   thing in the way, nothing else. The viewer should be able to solve the room half a beat before the party does.
2. **One twist per room, and it's physical.** Co-op that read instantly in half 1 was bodies: the trampoline catch, the human
   chain. Beam-ordering is abstract. Each hero owns one physical verb per room.
3. **The enemies are already here.** The design doc's own rule (the Eraser threatens fixtures, bugs threaten functions) now runs
   through the Talos rooms instead of starting at 107. The gap exists because the Eraser ate the bridge. The beam keeps failing
   because a bug is sitting on the receiver. In the Talos rooms bugs are only *shooed*; the battle is where they're *fixed*.
   That's the escalation, and the bugs no longer come from nowhere.

## The spine (what a viewer should be able to retell)

They land in the sunlit ruins. The road ahead has been rubbed out, and the machinery that should bridge it keeps glitching
because bugs are crawling over it. They shoo the bugs, cross, and on the stage the bugs turn and face them. You can't fight
bugs, so they debug them: each bug becomes a feature and joins. Then the Eraser rises and rubs out every structure, leaving the
colors they painted hanging in the air. From one spot, the colors and the Eraser's own trails spell 画: erasing was drawing all
along. Jade asks what she needs, and types `> you`. The Eraser joins as UNDO. With nine on the team they rebuild the world in
parallel, the bugs become the cart they ship in, and the Eraser fixes the last bug (the stuck 99%). Reality levels up again.

## The look: a diorama, not a game capture [art-dir]

The keyframes read as tilt-shift miniatures: shallow focus, low camera, big sky, one warm key, toy light. The render reads as a
game capture. Commit to the diorama and the voxels get forgiven the way the pixels in half 1 do. Rules per shot:

- Camera at hero chest height or lower, except the designed overheads (the swarm, the world map, the drop-2 aerial).
- One key light (golden hour, behind-left in Act III) and a cool fill; long shadows; a faked sunbeam only on the two glamour frames.
- Two colors per frame (sand and sky, or paper and one accent); everything else desaturated toward them.
- One focal subject per frame, ≥1/3 of the frame height, or one bright far point on an empty diagonal (the receiver).
- Depth layers: a foreground element (plate, column base, stele), the heroes, the far goal. Sky ≥1/3 of exteriors.
- Clutter: at most three columns in frame. The current Talos frames have ten.
- DOF: strong tilt-shift falloff on wide exteriors and on the lock; focus pulls on story beats.
- Master at 1080p with motion blur (`--mblur 8 --shutter 0.33`).
