The rule that holds it together

Nothing appears except by a stroke

一笔一画 means stroke by stroke, the way every kid learns to write Chinese. Here it is also the level design: 一 is a floor, 丨 a pillar, 丿 a slide, 丶 a stepping stone, all drawn in true stroke order from the Make Me a Hanzi dataset. Every change of world is drawn by a stroke or performed by the camera. No plain crossfades.

Your two pencils are the chorus. The pink-eraser pencil draws graphite: fixtures, the base layer (打底). The blue-eraser pencil draws in every color: functions, the color (上色). The world stays graphite and plaster until the rap line Fixtures 打底 functions 上色, when color floods all of it.

Engine test · real renderer, deterministic, ~0.1 s per frame on the 3090

The turn

The pixel game and the 3D world are the same scene. The 2D look is an orthographic side camera with a palette shader; the turn swings the camera and blends the projection, so it reads as the flat world opening up. The heroes here are rough placeholders; the real sprites below drop in next.

Known gaps I'll fix overnight: sun glare at the end of the turn, flat sand terrain in wide shots, placeholder heroes, grass that reads as gravel.
